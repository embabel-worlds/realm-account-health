1. The data

Every figure is read when this page loads, or when you press Refresh, from three systems through the world's knowledge graph: the CRM (accounts, deals, notes), the helpdesk (support cases) and billing (invoices, subscriptions). They meet on one node per company, CustomerAccount, which each system's realm keys by the company's web domain. Nothing is copied or stored by this app. The source realms cache a read for one to five minutes, so "now" can be up to five minutes old.

2. The questions

First, who is an account. A company becomes an account when one of the three systems' realms keys it, which happens when that system is read. So before anything else the page invokes the views listed under "Which views find the companies" below — by default OdooCompanies, LagoCustomers and ChatwootCompanies. These are the only product names on this page and they are a setting: a world with a different CRM lists that realm's view instead.

Then it invokes fifteen saved views from the account-health realm, all without parameters, and joins their rows by accountKey in the browser. Ten are plain facts:

  • HealthOpenCasesByAccount — open and pending support cases per account: count, high priority, longest thread, oldest opened, subjects.
  • HealthResolvedCasesByAccount — resolved cases per account and when the last was closed.
  • HealthReceivablesByAccount — unpaid invoices per account, how many failed collection, the amount owed.
  • HealthBillingByAccount — every invoice issued per account and how many were paid.
  • HealthRecurringByAccount — active subscriptions per account: amount per billing period and next renewal.
  • HealthPipelineByAccount — deals per account: open pipeline, open deal names, next close date, won and lost counts.
  • HealthCrmRecordByAccount — whether the CRM has a record of the account at all.
  • HealthCompanyNotesForOpenCases, HealthDealNotesForOpenCases, HealthContactNotesForOpenCases — for accounts with an open case only: the text and date of every CRM note on the company, on its deals and on its people.

Five are conclusions and judgements, and they are where the reasons on a card come from:

  • HealthAtRiskAccounts — reads the derived label AtRiskAccount. A rule set in the realm (rules/at-risk.yml) concludes which accounts are at risk, one independent clause per reason, each recording its own value. This page does not decide those reasons; it words them. They are computed when asked and stored nowhere, so an account leaves the label when its last reason stops being true.
  • HealthCaseTriage — a model reads what the customer wrote when raising each open case and judges impact (blocked, degraded, inconvenienced, asking) and mood (frustrated, neutral, positive). Kept for six hours, so it is as of its last run.
  • HealthCrmAwareness — a model reads every CRM note on an account beside its open cases and says whether sales is aware or unaware of the topic. Kept for six hours.
  • HealthSharedIssues — reads the derived relationship SHARES_ISSUE: two accounts each have an open case in the same product area (the helpdesk's own area-* labels).
  • HealthPossiblyWithheld — reads the derived relationship POSSIBLY_WITHHELD_OVER: an unpaid invoice and a still-open case on one account, where the case was raised before the invoice fell due.

The map. Every account in either inbox is a dot. Its area is the money at stake, so a dot twice the size is twice the money. Red needs attention, amber is worth a look, green is good news. A pulsing dot is a customer the model judged to be blocked or degraded and frustrated. Positions mean nothing beyond the side they are on. Click a dot to open the account.

What each label means. An account lands in Needs attention when any of these is true, and the card lists every one that is:

  • Blocked / hurting and frustrated — the model judged a case's impact as blocked or degraded and the customer's tone as frustrated.
  • Sales can't see this — it has an open case, the CRM has a record of it, and two tests agree that the CRM does not mention it. One is literal and done in this page: no CRM note shares a distinctive word with the case's subject (four letters or more, ignoring the vocabulary any software business uses). The other is the model's HealthCrmAwareness judgement. Each is wrong sometimes, about different accounts — the model once called a customer unaware whose notes said "custom reporting needs" while their case asked for a report builder — so a card needs both, and always shows you the notes themselves.
  • Selling into a problem becomes Open request while selling, in amber, when the model judged every open case to be a request or a nuisance and nobody frustrated: a 42-reply thread asking for dark mode is not a customer in trouble.
  • Others have this too and Marked high priority only ever add to a card that is there for another reason.
  • Selling into a problem — an open case and an open deal at the same time (rule set).
  • Not paying, and waiting on us — an unpaid invoice and an open case at the same time.
  • Payment failed — billing tried to collect an invoice and could not.
  • Renews soon with a case open — a subscription renews within 120 days and a case is open.
  • Nobody owns this customer — an open case, no record in the CRM and no invoice in billing.
  • Billed, but nobody owns them — billing invoices them and the CRM has no record.
  • Deal past its close date — an open deal whose expected close date has passed. This only ever ADDS to a card that is there for another reason. Accounts where it is the only thing wrong are counted in one line under the inboxes, because a slipped date is CRM housekeeping and not a customer in trouble.
  • Owes us — an unpaid invoice, with nothing else wrong.

An account with none of those lands in Good news when any of these is true: clear runway (an open deal that is not past its close date, no open case, nothing owed), easy renewal (renews within 120 days, no open case, nothing owed), just fixed (resolved cases and none open), pays every invoice (three or more invoices, all paid), room to grow (won before, nothing open now, still paying). Cards are ranked by money at stake: recurring amount per billing period plus open pipeline plus amount owed. Those can be in different currencies; the ranking adds the numbers as they are and the card shows each with its own currency. Pipeline is in the CRM's company currency.

The ask bar. Typing filters the cards already on screen. Pressing Enter sends your words, unchanged, to gateway.kg.ask, which writes a read-only graph query, runs it and returns rows. The query it wrote is shown under the answer so you can check it. "Done" (e) hides a card in this browser until the account's reasons change; it changes nothing in any system.

3. When it looks wrong

  • Loading takes a few seconds longer than it might. The thirteen views are read one after another on purpose: read at once, views that reach the same records can come back empty or short while reporting success, and this page would then call a troubled account good news.
  • An empty inbox with no warning means the three systems were read and nothing matched.
  • A yellow notice above the inboxes means one of the ten views could not be read. Signals that depend on it are withheld rather than guessed — in particular no account is called good news on the strength of a system that did not answer.
  • An account you expect is missing: it appears only once some system's realm has keyed it to a CustomerAccount. If a notice says a source view could not be read, companies known only to that system are absent.
  • An answer from the ask bar that looks off: open "How this was answered" and read the query. A plain-English question can be read more than one way.

4. What made this possible

  • None of the ten views names a product. They are written against a shared vocabulary (SupportCase, BillingInvoice, Opportunity) that each product's realm attaches to, so replacing the helpdesk changes no view and nothing on this page.
  • The CRM holds https://www.acmecorp.com, the helpdesk acmecorp.com and billing https://acmecorp.com. One declared normalization on the account type makes those one key; no system's realm cleans the value itself.
  • There is no pipeline, warehouse or sync job. Each view is a graph query whose far side is fetched from the product's own API when the query runs.
